Privacy policy

Open Prompt Manager keeps your prompt library on your computer. Nothing is uploaded unless you choose to publish a prompt to this catalog. The extension does not use analytics, advertising, or an account for its core features.

This policy covers the Chrome and Firefox extension and this website (Open Prompt Database). Both are made by the same independent developer, Jonathan Bertholet.

Stays on your device

  • Prompt titles, content, tags, and folders
  • Settings, shortcuts, and which sites you enabled
  • Pinned / remembered chat-input locators (not your chats)
  • JSON backups you export yourself

Leaves your device only if you ask

  • Import copies one catalog prompt into your local library
  • Share publishes a prompt you chose, under your public handle
  • Report sends a reason (and a hashed IP) for abuse review

Who this covers

Open Prompt Manager is the Chrome and Firefox extension that stores prompts locally and can insert them into AI chat sites you enable. Source: github.com/jonathanbertholet/promptmanager. Store listing: Chrome Web Store.

Open Prompt Database is this public catalog and API, hosted on Cloudflare at openpromptdatabase.com.

What the extension stores locally

Core use does not require an account, email address, or sign-in. Data lives in your browser’s extension storage on your profile.

  • Library — titles, content, tags, folders, local ids, and optional catalog ids (opd:) for prompts you imported or published.
  • Settings — launcher mode, theme, tag order, keyboard shortcut, append-vs-overwrite, enabled assistant sites, dismissed banners.
  • Site helpers — CSS selectors and hostnames for custom websites you pin, and for inputs the extension has successfully used. These find the chat box. They are not a copy of the page, your chats, or your browsing history.

If you use sharing, a few keys may be stored in Chrome Sync or Firefox Sync so they follow your browser profile: a random publish token, your public handle, and whether publishing is enabled. Your prompt library is not synced this way. Use export / import to copy prompts to another device.

Uninstalling the extension deletes this storage from that browser profile.

Permissions

Site access is optional. The extension does not require host permissions to install or update.

  • storage — save your library and settings on this device
  • sidePanel — show the side panel
  • tabs — know the current tab so you can insert a prompt or open an assistant
  • scripting — inject the in-page panel on sites you allowed, and the “save selection as prompt” dialog
  • activeTab — work with the tab you are using
  • contextMenus — copy a prompt or save selected text as a new prompt
  • Optional host access — assistant sites you enable, and (if you grant it) this catalog for one-click import

Optional <all_urls> in the manifest only lets your browser ask for a specific site when you pin a custom website. The extension does not receive access to every site unless you grant origins yourself.

On a granted site, the content script looks for the chat input so it can insert a prompt you picked. Page content, chat history, and cookies from those sites are not uploaded here. After you insert a prompt, that assistant’s own privacy policy applies.

What we do not do

  • No analytics, advertising, or usage-tracking SDKs in the extension
  • No sale of personal information
  • No login or email requirement for the local library
  • No remote upload of your full backup
  • No reading of your AI chats for training or telemetry
  • No use of your data to determine creditworthiness

Open Prompt Database (optional)

None of this happens unless you use the catalog.

Import

Add to Open Prompt Manager copies the selected prompt (title, content, tags, catalog id) into your local library. The site may increment an import count on that row — a counter, not a copy of your library.

Share / publish

  • Sends the prompt you chose (title, content, tags) to the catalog API
  • Publishes it under your public handle (for example @warm_ridge_491)
  • Authenticates with a random publish token (a UUID in the extension). It is not your Google account. Treat it like a password for your catalog identity
  • Cloudflare Turnstile may run to block bots

Firefox requests optional consent before sending publisher identity or a prompt for publication. You can revoke this in Firefox’s add-on permissions without losing local library features or catalog imports. Chrome and Firefox sync services are separate, so publisher identity does not transfer automatically.

Chrome may load assistant icons from provider sites or Google’s favicon service, which receives the requested hostname. Firefox uses bundled icons and a local generic fallback.

Published prompts are public. Do not publish API keys, passwords, private documents, or personal data. You can unpublish a prompt you uploaded. Sharing an imported community prompt publishes your copy, not the original author’s row.

Handle

No email is required. You pick (or accept a suggested) display handle. Handles are public. The server stores a hash of your publish token with that handle — not a name, phone number, or payment card.

Reports

Reports store the reason, optional detail, and a hashed IP address for abuse prevention. Turnstile may run on that form.

Website visitors

  • Pages and API responses are served by Cloudflare (Workers, D1, and related edge services)
  • Cloudflare may process IP address, user agent, and security signals to run the site, rate-limit abuse, and stop bots
  • Theme preference can be stored in localStorage on your device
  • Write actions (register, publish, report) may use Cloudflare Turnstile

You do not need an account to browse or copy prompts. Community entries are unverified — review them before pasting into any assistant.

Processors

  • Your browser — holds the local library
  • Mozilla Firefox / Mozilla Add-ons — runs and signs the Firefox extension; optional Firefox Sync for publisher keys
  • Google Chrome / Chrome Web Store — runs the extension; optional Chrome Sync for publisher keys
  • Cloudflare — hosts the catalog, API, Turnstile, and infrastructure logs
  • AI assistant sites — receive prompt text only after you insert or paste it there

Retention

  • Local data — until you delete it or uninstall
  • Published prompts — until you unpublish, or they are removed for abuse or legal reasons
  • Abuse reports — as needed to review and protect the catalog
  • Rate-limit records — short-lived (hours)
  • Cloudflare logs — according to Cloudflare’s own retention

Your choices and rights

  • Export or delete prompts in the extension; uninstall to remove local storage for that profile
  • Turn off catalog publishing in Open Prompt Database settings
  • Unpublish prompts you uploaded
  • Revoke site permissions in Settings → Permissions (or your browser’s extension details)
  • Ask us to help with a published prompt or handle using the contacts below

Depending on where you live (for example GDPR or CCPA), you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or restrict processing of personal data we hold on the catalog (handle, published prompts, reports). Email us. We do not sell personal information.

If you are in the EEA/UK, local library data is processed on your device at your instruction. Catalog processing is based on your request (publish / import / report) and on legitimate interests in running a public catalog and stopping abuse.

Children

These products are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

International processing

The catalog is served on Cloudflare’s global network. Information you publish or submit may be processed in the United States and other countries where Cloudflare operates.

Changes

We will update this policy when the products change in a way that affects privacy. The effective date at the top will change. Material changes will be reflected on this page and, when practical, in the extension changelog.
